# Lubec rules

These are the rules of Lubec, the tabletop game the group plays.
They cover the core dice procedure, Stats and Saves, classes, skills, Stress and Panic, Trauma and death, and a complete set of combat rules.
A group can play the whole game, including any fight, from this document with pen, paper and dice.

The document has two parts.
The first part is the rules for everything: "Core procedure" through "Effects, time and clocks".
The second part is combat: "What a character brings to a fight" through "Worked examples".

Every section has its own heading, so a rule can be cited by the name of its heading.
Where a rule is still being decided, the section says so in a note headed **Not yet decided**.
Until it is decided, the GM rules on it at the table.

## Core procedure

### When to roll

Roll only when an action is risky, consequential and genuinely uncertain.
Good questions, useful tools, sound plans or a strong position in the story can remove the need for a roll.

### Stat Checks

A **Stat Check** tests one of the five Stats.
Roll d100: equal to or under the Stat succeeds.
A failed Stat Check normally gives +1 Stress (+2 for an Overachiever).

### Saves

A **Save** tests one of the three Saves.
Roll d100: equal to or under the Save succeeds.
A failed Save gives +1 Stress.

### Opposed Checks

When two characters work against each other, both make the same kind of Check:

- Both roll d100 equal to or under the relevant Stat.
- If only one succeeds, that character wins.
- If both succeed, the higher successful roll wins.
- If both succeed on the same number, the higher Stat wins.
- If both fail, nothing changes.
- If the rolls and the Stats are both tied, the existing position or possession holds.

Grapples and takedowns use opposed Strength, and disarms use opposed Handle (see "Grappling, takedowns and disarming").
A race to a place has its own rule (see "Racing someone to a place").

## Dice and rolls

### Reading the dice

Lubec uses a d100 (two d10s, tens and ones) for almost every roll.
Read `00` as 100.
It also uses a d20 for Panic Checks, a d10 for the Death Table, Morale and which side of the body a hit lands on, a d4 for timers, and a d2 where a wound row offers two wounds.

### Rolling under

Some rolls must come in **strictly under** their number, and some succeed **at or under** it.

| Roll | Succeeds when the d100 is |
|---|---|
| Initiative | strictly under your Speed |
| Attack roll | strictly under your attack number |
| Defense roll | strictly under the Defense you put on that line |
| Catch roll | strictly under the catch chance |
| Strength Roll for Impact | at or under your Strength |
| Evasive Movement Speed roll | at or under your Speed |
| Stat Checks and Saves (Handle Check, Body Save and so on) | at or under the Stat or Save |

Strictly under means an attack number of 60 hits on `01` to `59`.
A number of 0 or less can never succeed.

### Tens digit

Many rules use the **tens digit** of a successful roll.
`01`-`09` has a tens digit of 0, `10`-`19` has 1, `20`-`29` has 2, and so on up to `90`-`99`, which has 9.

### Advantage and Disadvantage

On a Check or Save, **Advantage** means roll twice and keep the better roll, and **Disadvantage** means roll twice and keep the worse one.
For a roll-under Check, the better roll is the lower one.

Attack rolls work out success first and Quality second:

- **Attack with Advantage:** roll twice. If either roll hits, use the higher roll that hits. Any hit beats a miss.
- **Attack with Disadvantage:** roll twice. If either roll misses, the attack misses. If both hit, use the lower roll.

Disadvantage does not stack: two sources of Disadvantage are still one Disadvantage.
The same is true of Advantage.

### Criticals

A double (`11`, `22`, `33` and so on up to `99`) is a **critical**.
On a successful attack roll a critical changes the attack's Quality (see "Criticals on attack").
On any other roll it is only a critical: it changes nothing else.

### Table modifiers

The GM may add a one-off adjustment to any roll for the situation, and says why: "-20, you are swinging in the dark."
Common adjustments are -10 and -20, but any number is allowed.
It lasts for that one roll unless the GM says otherwise.

## Stats and Saves

### The five Stats

| Stat | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Strength** | Force, lifting, carrying, pushing, pulling, dragging, forcing, wrestling and bodily control. |
| **Speed** | Running, sneaking, pursuit, balance, evasion, reaction and whole-body quickness. |
| **Intellect** | Understanding, research, memory, deduction, repair, technical work and figuring out systems. |
| **Handle** | Aiming, throwing, catching, striking, grabbing, sleight of hand and controlled physical action under pressure. |
| **Charm** | The fifth Stat. See the note below. |

> **Not yet decided:** what Charm covers.
> The rules name it as a Stat, and classes raise or lower it, but they do not yet say which actions it is rolled for.

### The three Saves

| Save | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Sanity** | Facing the impossible or reality-breaking. |
| **Fear** | Immediate terror, pressure, pursuit, entrapment and shock. |
| **Body** | Cold, exhaustion, poison, illness, drowning, concussion and other bodily threats. |

A failed Save adds Stress (+1).
Fear is a Save; it is not rolled to resist Panic (see "Panic Check").

### Rolling up a character

1. Roll 4d20 separately for each Stat. The range is 4 to 80 and the average is 42. The wide spread is deliberate: strong natural strengths and strong natural weaknesses are what Lubec wants.
2. Roll 2d10 + 10 separately for each Save.
3. Choose a class after rolling: pick the class that best fits the kid you rolled. Class is never assigned at random.
4. Apply the class's Stat and Save modifiers, and take its starting skills (see "Classes").
5. Start with 2 Stress, 2 Minimum Stress and 0 Trauma.

No Stat or Save goes below 0.

> **Not yet decided:** how adult NPCs' Stats are rolled.

## Classes

### Family and class

Family supplies a kid's pressures, relationships, access, resources, inherited secrets and reasons to care about Lubec.
Family does not decide class.

Class is a broad posture for how a kid tends to face problems.
Skills are where specific competence comes from.
Class does not dictate personality.

## Skills

### How skills work

A skill is the knowledge, craft, technique or training a kid has in one field.
When you make a Stat Check or Save and you have a skill that fits, add its bonus to the number you roll under.

- A **Trained** skill adds +10.
- An **Expert** skill adds +15, and needs at least one of its listed skills first.
- Skill bonuses never stack: if two of your skills fit, use the better one.
- Scrapping, Boxing, Firearms and Slingshot / Archery are fighting and shooting skills: they count only in a fight (see "Fighting and shooting skills").

### No skill

Having no skill that fits does not mean you know nothing, and it usually does not stop you trying.
It means you get no bonus.
Depending on how hard the task is, the GM may have you roll with Disadvantage, or rule that you cannot attempt it at all.

## Stress, Panic, Trauma and death

### Stress

**Stress** is built-up pressure, and it drives Panic.
A character starts with 2 Stress and 2 Minimum Stress, and Stress never falls below their **Minimum Stress**.

Stress comes from:

- a failed Save: +1 Stress;
- a failed Stat Check: +1 Stress (+2 for an Overachiever);
- injuries, and witnessing injuries: a wound row can give Stress ("Gain 1 Stress");
- Panic table rows;
- class trauma responses, which can give Stress to friends who witness something (see "The five classes").

Stress does not decide a player character's considered choices.
Players decide whether their characters keep fighting, flee, surrender, confess or do anything else, whenever they are still able to choose.
Panic can force involuntary reactions.

> **Not yet decided:** what to do when Minimum Stress rises above current Stress.
> The recommendation is to lift Stress up to the new Minimum Stress, since Stress may never be below it.

### Stress in combat

In a fight, Stress comes from injuries and from witnessing injuries, not from failed swings:

- Failed attack, Defense, catch, Strength, initiative and Evasive Movement rolls give no Stress.
- A wound row can give Stress ("Gain 1 Stress").
- The Checks and Saves that wound rows call for give Stress like any other failed Check or Save.

### Panic Check

Panic is for very severe events.
The GM decides at the table when to call for a Panic Check.

Panic is its own roll, separate from Fear: a Panic Check is not a Fear Save, and Fear is not rolled to resist Panic.
Failing a Fear Save adds Stress, which makes a later Panic Check harder, but it does not make you Panic by itself.

To make a **Panic Check**, roll d20 against your current Stress:

- Higher than your current Stress: you hold it together.
- Equal to or lower than your current Stress: you Panic, and the same d20 picks your row on the Panic table.

A failed Panic Check gives no Stress by itself; any Stress on the Panic row still applies.

| 8 | **LOSS OF CONFIDENCE.** Gain a new Condition: Choose one Skill and lose that Skill's bonus. |

| 12 | **HAUNTED.** Gain a new Condition: Something starts visiting the character at night. In their dreams. Out of the corner of their eye. And soon it will start making demands. |
| 13 | **DEATH WISH.** For the next 24 hours, whenever encountering a stranger or known enemy, make a Sanity Save or immediately attack them. |

| 17 | **SPIRALING.** Gain a new Condition: Panic Checks are at Disadvantage. |
| 18 | **COMPOUNDING PROBLEMS.** Roll twice on this table. Increase your Minimum Stress by 1. |
| 19 | **HEART ATTACK / SHORT CIRCUIT (ANDROIDS).** Reduce Maximum Wounds by 1. Disadvantage on all rolls for 1d10 hours. Increase Minimum Stress by 1. |
| 20 | **RETIRE.** Roll up a new character to play. |

In Lubec, "crew" and "crewmembers" mean the kid's group.

How to run the rows:

- A lasting row (a Condition, or Loss of Confidence) stays on the character as an effect until it is removed.
- When a row calls for a Fear Save and it fails, the row's Stress counts on top of the usual +1 Stress for a failed Save.
- Rows that trigger a later check (Coward, Frightened, Suspicious, Death Wish) are run by the player: the row text is enough, and the player makes the check when it comes up.
- Doomed is handled by the player, as written.
- Rows that give Stress to the group (Jumpy, Rage, Deflated) are applied to the whole group by the GM.
- Reduce and increase Stress and Minimum Stress as the row says; Stress never drops below Minimum Stress.

### Stress Recovery

After a full night's sleep somewhere reasonably safe (a Long Rest), make one Recovery Save against your lowest Save.

- On a success, reduce Stress by the ones digit of the roll.
- On a failure, Stress does not change.
- Make at most one Recovery Save a night.
- Ordinary recovery never takes Stress below 2, or below Minimum Stress if that is higher.

Recovery is deliberately procedural.
The game does not reduce Stress for self-care, altruism, solidarity, truth-telling or other roleplayed virtues.

### Trauma and the Death Table

A character rolls on the **Death Table** when:

- their Trauma reaches 4 (5 for a Tough); or
- a wound calls for it: an Immediate Death Save, or a Death Save countdown that runs out.

The GM rolls the d10 hidden: they shake it in a cup and leave it face down.
It is revealed only when someone spends a turn checking the character's vitals.

### Death Table

| d10 | Result |
|---:|---|
| 0 | You are unconscious. You wake up in 2d10 minutes. Add 1d5 to your Minimum Stress. |
| 1-2 | You are unconscious and dying. You die in 1d5 rounds without intervention. |
| 3-4 | You are comatose. Only extraordinary measures can return you to the waking world. |
| 5-9 | You have died. Roll up a new character. |

Unconsciousness can come from a wound row (knocked unconscious) or from the Death Table.
Either way, the character is Out of the fight.

> **Not yet decided:** whether to keep this table (Mothership's Death Table, with 1d5 Minimum Stress in place of lost maximum Health, since Lubec has no Health), soften it, or replace it.

### Morale

Morale is for NPCs.
Player characters always decide for themselves whether to keep fighting, flee or surrender, and players can always choose to stop.

A living NPC checks Morale as soon as it reaches 2 Trauma.
Undead do not check Morale.
Roll d10:

| d10 | Morale result |
|---:|---|
| 1-3 | Flee |
| 4-6 | Surrender |
| 7-8 | Keep fighting |
| 9-10 | Call for help |

Calling for help is free: it costs the NPC nothing in the round.
Fleeing or surrendering does not have to end the fight; cruelty is possible.
A Morale check is what usually stops a fistfight from becoming lethal.
A kid who cannot do real damage stops by roleplay; there is no stalemate rule.

## Effects, time and clocks

### Effects and how long they last

A wound, a Panic result or anything else that changes a character for a while is an **effect** on the character, and each effect notes what caused it.
Effects stack.

The game tracks what happens in fights, not how many days pass.
When an effect changes after a Long Rest (for example, a Severe Concussion stepping down from -40 Handle to -10), the player makes that change at the table.
A duration that is rolled (such as "2d10 minutes") is rolled when the effect starts and written on it; nobody counts it down.

### Long Rest

A **Long Rest** is a full night's sleep somewhere reasonably safe.
It is the same night that allows a Recovery Save (see "Stress Recovery").

### Clocks

A **clock** tracks something that builds up or wears down: a track of 1 to 10 pieces, moved up or down by hand.
A clock has a name, a description, what makes it go up, what makes it go down, what happens when it fills, and what happens when it empties.
A chase is run on a clock (see "Chases").

Injuries are effects, not clocks: an effect with only one or two steps of recovery is not worth a clock.

> **Not yet decided:** where the line between an effect and a clock falls, for example clocks only for recoveries of three or more steps.

What a character carries can lower Speed, and that lower Speed is the one used in a fight.

| What is on you | Speed |
|---|---|
| A heavy thing, and you failed its Strength check | -20 while it is on you |
| An extra heavy thing (you must pass its Strength check to carry it at all) | -20, always |
| Any thing that takes 8 slots | -20 |
| A full inventory: full backpack, full body slots and full hands | -10, no roll |

### Effective Handle

**Effective Handle** is your Handle with every effect applied, capped at 100.
It is the starting point for every attack and every Defense.

### Fighting and shooting skills

A skill adds to the attack number when it fits the attack.
A Trained skill adds +10 and an Expert skill adds +15.
Skill bonuses never stack: if you have two skills that fit, use the better one.

| Attack | Skills that add, best first |
|---|---|
| Punch, kick, blunt swing, slash, stab | Boxing (Expert, +15), Scrapping (Trained, +10) |
| Shooting a firearm | Firearms (Expert, +15) |
| Throwing | Throwing Arm (Expert, +15) |
| Slingshot or bow | Slingshot / Archery (Expert, +15) |

Boxing needs Scrapping or Athletics first.
Firearms needs Hunting first.
Throwing Arm needs Athletics first.
Slingshot / Archery needs Campcraft or Hunting first.

Scrapping, Boxing, Firearms and Slingshot / Archery count only in a fight: they never add to an ordinary Check outside combat.

A character with Scrapping or Boxing is **trained in a fighting skill**.
That matters for kicking with your hands full (see "Fisticuffs: punches and kicks").

> **Not yet decided:** whether an Expert shooting skill exists as intended, and if not, one will be added.
> Whether Hunting and Throwing Arm count as fighting or shooting skills (which would mean they count only in a fight).

### Classes in combat

| Class | Death Table at | A failed Stat Check gives | Trauma response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Working Kid | 4 Trauma | +1 Stress | **Steady Under Pressure.** Once per session, you may take Advantage on a Panic Check. |
| Nerd | 4 Trauma | +1 Stress | **Failed Sanity Spreads Stress.** Whenever you fail a Sanity Save, every Close Group friend who witnesses it gains 1 Stress. |
| Tough | 5 Trauma | +1 Stress | **Your Panic Triggers Fear.** Whenever you Panic, every Close Group friend who witnesses it must make a Fear Save. |
| Overachiever | 4 Trauma | +2 Stress | **Your Success Raises the Stakes.** When you and Close Group friends make Fear Saves against the same threat, each friend who fails gains 2 Stress instead of 1 if you succeed. |
| Popular Kid | 4 Trauma | +1 Stress | **Everyone Looks to You.** Whenever you fail a Fear Save, every Close Group friend who witnesses it gains 1 Stress. |

A failed Save always gives +1 Stress, whatever the class.

## Starting a fight

### Who is in the fight

The GM decides who is in the fight: the player characters and NPCs who are there when it starts.
Player characters fight NPCs, not each other.
An NPC can fight anyone, including another NPC.
Everyone can see what armor each combatant is wearing.

### Items on the ground

Things lying around that someone could grab, such as a dropped bat or a pile of dodgeballs, are **on the ground**.
The GM lists them one by one before the fight starts, and can add more at any time.
A thrown object that is not caught ends up on the ground too.

### Sneak Attacks

Before initiative is rolled, the GM may grant a **Sneak Attack**: one immediate attack by someone who caught the other side by surprise.

- The GM grants one Sneak Attack at a time, and it is resolved completely, wound and all, before the next one is granted or initiative is rolled.
- The attacker picks a target, a weapon and an attack, and rolls as in "The attack roll and Quality".
- If it hits, the target may defend on the spot: they put any amount of their own Handle, in tens, on Defense against the attacker and roll it as in "Rolling Defense". They may instead put their whole Handle on it and roll at +10 (Full Defense). This Defense costs them nothing for round 1.
- Then the hit goes on through hit location, Impact and the wound as usual.
- A wound from a Sneak Attack starts in round 1, so it can already lower the target's Speed for initiative.

Hitting a sleeping, tied up or unaware target is not a Sneak Attack: the GM rules on it outside the normal attack steps.

### Initiative

Once the GM calls for initiative, everyone in the fight rolls it once.
Initiative is rolled **once per fight**, not every round.

Roll d100.
It succeeds if it comes in strictly under your Speed.
Rank everyone like this:

1. Everyone who succeeded goes before everyone who failed.
2. Among those who succeeded, the **higher** roll goes first.
3. Among those who failed, the **lower** roll goes first.
4. If two rolls are still level, the higher Speed goes first.
5. If two people are still tied after that, both roll d100 again and rank that roll the same way, as many times as it takes.

The first in the order is the **fastest** and the last is the **slowest**.

Example: Mirelda (Speed 58) rolls `41`, a success.
Rex (Speed 45) rolls `44`, a success.
Tommy (Speed 35) rolls `60`, a failure.
The order, fastest first, is Rex, Mirelda, Tommy.

## The combat round

### Round order

Every round has two halves: everyone **declares**, then everything **resolves**.

1. **Declare, slowest first.** Starting with the slowest and ending with the fastest, each combatant declares their movement and their action for the round, out loud. Faster characters hear what slower ones are doing before they commit.
2. **Movement resolves.** Once everyone has declared, all movement happens.
3. **Attacks resolve, fastest first.** Each attack is resolved in full, one at a time, fastest first, in one pass. Then the next round begins with new declarations.

Initiative order does not change from round to round.

### Declaring

On your turn to declare, say:

- **Your movement:** move or stay, run (full or partial), Evasive Movement, stand up, pick up an item, or leave the fight (see "Movement").
- **Your action:** an attack (target, weapon and attack) with any Defense lines, or no attack and only Defense (see "Splitting Handle between Attack and Defense"). Attack and Defense are both part of your action: a round gives you your movement and your action, and nothing else.

Everyone can see each declaration, including how much Defense is on each line and who it names, and who is going for which item on the ground.

Some movement uses up your action, and then you have no attack and no Defense that round:

| Movement | Attack this round? | Defense this round? |
|---|---|---|
| Move or stay | Yes | Yes |
| Partial run | Yes | Yes |
| Full run | No | No |
| Evasive Movement | No | No |
| Standing up (failed or no Speed roll) | No | No |
| Standing up (successful Speed roll) | Yes | Yes |
| Pick up an item | Yes | Yes |
| Leave the fight | No | No: you are Out |

### When effects start

Every effect of a hit starts **the round after the hit**.
A target who is wounded this round still moves, attacks and defends as declared for the rest of this round.
Being put **Out** is the exception: it happens at once (see "Out").

Timers count rounds.
A "+1 Trauma in d4 turns" or "Death Save in d4 turns" timer that starts in round *r* runs out at the end of round *r* + d4.
A d4 of 1 still gives the wounded character the next round's action.

### Choosing again

By the time your attack comes up, something you declared may be gone.
You can change your declaration only for what has gone:

- If your declared target is Out, you may pick a new target.
- If your declared weapon is no longer in your hand, you may pick another weapon or attack you can make now, or pick up an item from the ground with your action (it goes into a free hand and your turn ends), or pass.
- If both are still there, you must attack as declared.

Your Defense lines stay as declared either way.

## Movement

### Distance

One **unit** is 10 meters.
Estimate distances to the nearest 10 meters unless a close finish matters.

### Move or stay

Moving a short way or staying put is the ordinary choice.
Walking covers a fixed distance and leaves your action free.

### Running

- **Full run (sprinting):** you run your full Speed in units, but you make no attack and roll no Defense that round. Speed 65 runs 6 units, 60 meters.
- **Partial run:** you run half your Speed and keep your full attack and Defense. Halve the Speed first, then take units: Speed 65 runs on Speed 32, so 3 units, 30 meters.

Wounds that limit movement give **half Movement**: halve the distance you can walk or run.

### Evasive Movement

**Evasive Movement** is ducking, weaving and diving for cover so you are harder to hit from a distance.

- It uses your action for the round: you cannot shoot, attack, reload, use a skill or declare Defense that round.
- You move at half speed.
- When movement resolves, roll Speed (d100 at or under your Speed).
- On a success, anyone making a ranged attack at you this round takes a Handle penalty from your roll: 10 times half the roll's tens digit, rounded down.
- A failed roll gives no penalty.

| Successful Speed roll | Penalty to ranged attacks at you |
|---|---|
| `01`-`19` | 0 |
| `20`-`39` | -10 |
| `40`-`59` | -20 |
| `60`-`79` | -30 |
| `80`-`99` | -40 |

The penalty counts against guns, projectiles and thrown objects.
A dodgeball is the exception: it is answered by Defense, not Evasive Movement.

A wound that says **No Evasive Movement** stops you from choosing it while the wound lasts.

Example: Mirelda (Speed 58) moves evasively and rolls `47`.
It succeeds; the tens digit is 4, half of 4 is 2, so ranged attacks at her this round take -20.

### Standing up from prone

Standing up after being knocked down takes your movement and your action for the round.
Roll Speed first (d100 at or under your Speed): on a success, standing up takes only your movement and you keep your action.
On a failure you have no attack and no Defense that round, because both are part of your action.

Knocked down means you fell down.
Nobody holds you down unless they declared a grapple.
While you are down, kicks and blunt swings at you use the "target down" hit location tables (see "Where the hit lands").

### Picking up items

You can declare that you are going for one item on the ground.
You need a free hand for it, both when you declare and when movement resolves.

When movement resolves, pickups are settled fastest first.
If someone faster took the item first, or your hands have filled since, you get nothing.
An item you pick up goes into your free hand.

### Leaving the fight

You can declare that you are leaving the fight.
A player character's Leave needs the GM's agreement: the GM can refuse it if leaving is not possible from where you are, and then you declare something else.
When movement resolves you are **Out** (see "Out").

## Attacks

### Weapons and their attacks

A weapon can only make the attacks it was made for: a knife can slash or stab, but it cannot make a blunt attack.
Each kind of attack has its own skill, hit location table, wound table and kind of armor.

| Attack | Skill | Adds the Strength Roll | Hit location table | Wound table | Armor that counts | Defense? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Punch | Fighting | Yes | Punch | Fisticuffs | Blunt | Yes |
| Kick | Fighting | Yes | Kick (or Kick / stomp, target down) | Fisticuffs | Blunt | Yes |
| Blunt swing (bat, club, pipe) | Fighting | Yes | Blunt swing (or target down) | Blunt Force | Blunt | Yes |
| Slash (knife, glass, hatchet, axe) | Fighting | Yes | Slash | Cutting | Cut | Yes |
| Stab (knife, spike, point) | Fighting | Yes | Stab | Piercing | Pierce | Yes |
| Throw, blunt (rock, bottle, ball) | Throwing | Yes | Thrown object | Thrown Objects | Blunt | Yes |
| Throw, edged (thrown knife or hatchet) | Throwing | Yes | Thrown object | Cutting | Cut | Yes |
| Throw, pointed (thrown spike) | Throwing | Yes | Thrown object | Piercing | Pierce | Yes |
| Shoot (firearm) | Shooting | No | Firearm | Gunshot | Gun | No |

A slash and a stab are different attacks: you declare which one you are making.
A weapon can name its own wound table, and then it uses that one instead: a dodgeball uses the Dodgeball table.

Slingshot and bow shots are projectiles: Defense does not work against them.

> **Not yet decided:** which hit location table and wound table slingshot and bow shots use.

### Fisticuffs: punches and kicks

Every character can fight barehanded.
This is **Fisticuffs**, and it has two attacks, Punch and Kick, both with base Impact 0.

- With at least one hand free, you can punch or kick.
- With both hands full, you can kick only if you are trained in a fighting skill (Scrapping or Boxing). Otherwise you have nothing to fight with until you drop something.

Dropping something you are holding is free.

### Hands and holding weapons

A weapon must be in your hand to attack with it.
Each hand holds one small or 1-slot thing.
Anything bigger, and anything heavy or extra heavy, takes both hands.

What moving things costs, in actions (you get one action a round):

| Move | Cost |
|---|---|
| Body slot to hand | Free |
| Hand to body slot | 1 action |
| Into or out of the backpack | 2 actions |
| Drop from a hand or a body slot | Free |
| Drop from the backpack | 2 actions |
| Hand to worn (putting on armor) | 1 action |
| Body slot to worn | 2 actions |
| Taking armor off | What putting it on costs |
| Move a thing to the other hand | Free |

> **Not yet decided:** the rules for dropped weapons (what happens when a wound knocks a weapon loose, and getting it back).

### Heavy weapons and Minimum Strength

A weapon may have a **Minimum Strength**: the lowest Strength that uses it with no penalty.
Below it, every attack with the weapon takes a Handle penalty.
Any Strength below the minimum costs at least -10 Handle, and each lower tens band of Strength costs another -10.
There is no cap.

For an axe (Minimum Strength 35): Strength 35 and up, no penalty; 20-34, -10; 10-19, -20; 0-9, -30.

| Weapon | Minimum Strength |
|---|---|
| Sledgehammer | 50 |
| Pick-axe | 40 |
| Axe | 35 |
| Hatchet | 30 |
| Fist-sized rock (thrown) | 20 |

The axe and the pick-axe are heavy weapons.
There is no separate "too heavy to use" rule: the Handle penalty is what being too weak for a weapon costs.

> **Not yet decided:**
> the Minimum Strength of the splitting maul (which was 60 and is now heavier than the sledgehammer), the crowbar and the anchor;
> whether the axe and the pick-axe deal heavy slashing and heavy piercing, or a flat +4 Impact (a pick-axe is mostly a dangerous blunt attack);
> whether a heavy weapon needs a Strength check for every attack, and what happens if you put one down and pick it up again.

### Gloves and hand armor

Armor worn on a hand costs -10 Handle a layer on attacks made with that hand.
Armor that covers every layer (exclusive armor) counts as every layer of that hand.
A two-handed weapon, or Fisticuffs with both hands, takes the worse hand's penalty, never both added together.

### The attack number

Your **attack number** is what your attack roll must come in under.
Work it out like this:

1. Start with your Effective Handle.
2. Take off the weapon's Minimum Strength penalty, if any.
3. Take off the gloves penalty, if any.
4. Add the skill that fits the attack, if you have one.
5. Keep the result between 0 and 100. This is your **Handle pool** for the round.
6. Take off everything you put on Defense lines.
7. For a ranged attack, add the range modifier.
8. For a ranged attack at someone using Evasive Movement, take off their Evasive Movement penalty.
9. Apply any Advantage, Disadvantage or table modifier.

Example: Tommy has Handle 52 and Scrapping (+10), and swings a baseball bat, which has no Minimum Strength.
His Handle pool is 62.
He puts 20 on a Defense line, so his attack number is 42.

### Splitting Handle between Attack and Defense

When you declare an attack, you split your Handle pool between your attack and **Defense lines**:

- Each Defense line names one opponent and holds some of your Handle, in tens: 10, 20, 30 and so on.
- Whatever you put on Defense comes off your attack number.
- You must leave some Handle for the attack.
- A line only protects you against the person it names. If they never attack you, it is wasted, but it still came off your attack.
- Everyone can see the split.

If you use your action for Defense alone, with **no attack**, you can put your whole pool on Defense, and every Defense line you roll gets +10 (see "Full Defense").
If your movement used up your action, you have no Defense lines at all that round.

### Ranged attacks and range

For a ranged attack (a shot or a throw), the GM declares the range band when the attack comes up: **Short**, **Medium**, **Long** or **Extreme**.
Each ranged weapon has its own bands and its own Handle modifier for each band; the modifier is added to the attack number.

> **Not yet decided:** the distance limits of each band and the Handle modifier for each band, for each weapon.
> Until a weapon has its numbers, the GM sets the modifier for the band; the current playtest values for firearms are +10 at close range and +0 at Medium.

### Firearms and ammunition

- Firing uses one round of ammunition. With no round loaded, the gun cannot fire.
- Loading a gun is an action. It takes 1 action with the ammunition in your other hand (or that hand empty to fetch it), 2 with it in a body slot, and 3 with it in your backpack. Ammunition in a carrier cannot be used.
- You cannot load and fire in the same action.
- Opposed Defense never answers a firearm (see "What Defense works against").
- A firearm's Impact gets no Strength Roll.

### Throwing

- A weapon made to be thrown (a ball, a rock) is thrown as its attack.
- A weapon can also be marked "can be thrown". Then it has its own thrown Impact and the one kind of wound it does when thrown. A weapon that is blunt when thrown uses the Thrown Objects wound table; one that cuts when thrown uses Cutting, and one that pierces uses Piercing.
- A thrown attack adds the Strength Roll, like melee.
- The object leaves your hand when you roll. If you miss, it lands on the ground. If you hit and it is not caught, it ends up on the ground too.
- A thrown object has a **catch chance** (see "Catching thrown objects").

> **Not yet decided:** the numbers for thrown weapons. A weapon may make a thrown attack weaker than its swing (a thrown knife might be -1 Impact), and each thrown item's catch chance is set when it is made; neither has settled values yet.
> Until then, the draft guide for improvised objects is their **heft**, which works like base Impact:
>
> | Heft (added Impact) | Examples | Strength needed to throw it |
> |---:|---|---|
> | 0 | Empty can or bottle, apple, pebble, shoe | Any |
> | +1 | Fist-sized rock, full bottle, hammer, wrench | Any |
> | +2 | Brick, rock the size of a head, pipe wrench, hatchet thrown end over end | 30 or more |
> | +3 | Cinder block, small boulder, car battery; two hands, only at a target within reach of a step | 50 or more |
> | Too heavy | Toolbox, sack of cement, tire on its rim, anything heavier | Cannot be thrown |

### Stock weapons and armor

These are the game's ready-made weapons and armor.
Anything else is made up at the table using the same numbers.

| Weapon | Attacks | Base Impact | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Baseball bat | Blunt swing | +1 | Two hands (2 slots). |
| Pocketknife | Slash or stab | 0 | Small. |
| .38 revolver | Shoot | +1 | Holds 6 rounds. |
| Dad's shotgun | Shoot | +2 | Holds 2 shells. Two hands (4 slots). |
| Dodgeball | Throw | 0 | Dodgeball wound table. Catch chance 50%. Catching it puts the thrower Out. |
| Fist-sized rock | Throw | +1 | Thrown Objects wound table. Minimum Strength 20. Catch chance 10%. |

| Armor | Covers | Cut | Pierce | Blunt | Gun |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Leather work gloves | Both hands | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| Football shoulder pads | Torso, both upper arms | 1 | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| Snowmobile suit (exclusive: nothing else fits under it) | Torso, upper arms, forearms, waist, groin, thighs, shins | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 |

### The attack roll and Quality

On your turn, roll d100.
If it comes in strictly under your attack number, you **hit**.
Otherwise you miss, and the attack is over.

A hit has a **Quality**: the tens digit of the attack roll.
Quality is both how hard you hit and how far you can move where the hit lands, so Defense, which lowers Quality, cuts both.

| Attack roll that hits | Quality |
|---|---:|
| `01`-`09` | 0 |
| `10`-`19` | 1 |
| `20`-`29` | 2 |
| `30`-`39` | 3 |
| `40`-`49` | 4 |
| `50`-`59` | 5 |
| `60`-`69` | 6 |
| `70`-`79` | 7 |
| `80`-`89` | 8 |
| `90`-`99` | 9 |

A Quality 0 hit is still a hit.

Failing an attack roll never gives Stress.

> **Not yet decided:** whether a roll of 90-99 always misses, as in Mothership.
> Whether a missed melee attack should still give a small glancing result instead of a pure miss.

### Criticals on attack

If the attack roll hits and is a double (`11`, `22`, `33` ... `99`), it is a **critical**: the Quality is the tens digit of your **attack number** instead of the roll.
That one number counts for both how hard you hit and how far you can move the hit.

Example: Tommy's attack number is 42 and he rolls `33`.
It is a critical, so his Quality is 4 (the tens digit of 42), not 3.

## Defense and catching

### Defense lines

A **Defense line** is Handle you hold back to guard against one named opponent.
You declare your lines with your action, in tens, and everyone can see them.
When the person a line names attacks you and hits, you roll Defense with that line.
You may declare a line against each of several opponents, as long as you leave some Handle for your attack.
A line cannot be moved to another opponent once declared.
If someone you have no line against attacks you, you take the hit with no Defense.

### Full Defense

A character who uses their action for Defense alone, with **no attack**, can put their whole Handle pool on Defense, and rolls **every** Defense line at +10, to a top of 100.

A character whose movement used up their action (a full run, Evasive Movement, or standing up without a successful Speed roll) has no Defense at all that round.

### Rolling Defense

Defense is rolled only after the attack hits, by the defender, with the line that names the attacker.

1. Roll d100. It succeeds if it comes in strictly under the line's amount (plus 10 for Full Defense).
2. A successful Defense takes **its tens digit plus 1** off the attack's Quality. A failed Defense takes off nothing.
3. If that brings the Quality to 0 or below, the attack is fully **stopped**: no hit location, no Impact, no wound.
4. Otherwise the hit goes on with the Quality that is left.

Example: Rex hits Tommy with Quality 3.
Tommy has a 20 line against Rex and rolls `14`, a success: its tens digit is 1, plus 1, so it takes 2 off.
Rex's hit goes on with Quality 1.

Failing a Defense roll never gives Stress.

### What Defense works against

| Attack | Defense? |
|---|---|
| Punches, kicks and melee weapons | Yes |
| Thrown objects, including dodgeballs | Yes |
| Firearms | No |
| Projectiles: arrows, slingshot shots | No |

Against guns and projectiles your defense is Evasive Movement and cover.

### Catching thrown objects

When Defense stops a thrown object, you may be able to catch it:

- The Defense must take the hit to 0 or below.
- You need a free hand.
- The object must have a catch chance above 0%. An object at 0% cannot be caught.

Your **catch chance** is the object's own catch chance plus 10 for every point Defense took the Quality below 0, up to 100%.
Roll d100: if it comes in strictly under your catch chance, you catch it and it is in your hand.
If you miss the catch, the object drops to the ground, and the attack is still stopped.

Some objects, such as dodgeballs, are marked **catching this puts the thrower out**: if you catch it, the thrower is Out.

Example: Rex throws a dodgeball at Mirelda and hits with Quality 2.
Mirelda's 40 Defense line against Rex rolls `31`, which takes off 3 + 1 = 4, leaving -2.
Her catch chance is 50% plus 2 times 10, so 70%.
She rolls `44` and catches it, and Rex is Out.

### Left or right

Arms, hands, thighs, shins and feet come in pairs.
When the hit lands on one, roll d10: odd is the target's left, even is the target's right.

### Moving the hit

The attacker may move where the hit lands, along the body, as many steps as the Quality left after Defense.
Each step moved takes 1 off the Impact.
The attacker may stop anywhere within reach, not only at the farthest point.

The body is joined like this, one step per link:

- Head - Neck - Torso - Waist - Groin
- Torso - Upper arm - Forearm - Hand (each side)
- Waist - Thigh - Shin - Foot (each side)

So the head is 2 steps from the torso, the right hand is 3 steps from the torso, and the left foot is 4 steps from the torso (torso, waist, thigh, shin, foot).
Going from one arm to the other passes through the torso.

Example: Rex hits Mirelda with Quality 3 and the hit lands on her torso.
He can move it up to 3 steps: to her head (2 steps) for -2 Impact, or to her neck (1 step) for -1 Impact, or leave it on the torso.

## Impact

### The Strength Roll

When a punch, kick, melee weapon or throw hits, the attacker makes a **Strength Roll** once the hit's place is settled: d100 at or under Strength.
On a success, add the roll's tens digit to Impact: `01`-`09` adds 0, `10`-`19` adds 1, `20`-`29` adds 2, and so on.
On a failure it adds nothing.
A failed Strength Roll gives no Stress.

Firearms and projectiles make no Strength Roll.

### Working out Impact

**Impact** = Quality left after Defense + the weapon's base Impact + the Strength Roll - steps the hit was moved - armor at that spot against that kind of wound.

Impact picks the row on the wound table for that attack and that part of the body.

### Armor

Worn armor has a number for each kind of wound: cut, pierce, blunt and gun.
Where pieces overlap on one part of the body, their numbers add up.
The number for the attack's kind of armor comes off Impact at the part that was hit.

Each part of the body holds only so many layers of armor:

| Part | Layers |
|---|---:|
| Head, neck, forearm, hand, waist, groin, shin, feet | 2 |
| Torso, upper arm, thigh | 3 |

Ordinary armor takes one layer; exclusive armor takes every layer where it covers.

### Impact 0 or less

If Impact comes to 0 or less, the attack hits but causes no wound and nothing else: no Trauma, no Stress and no other effect.

## Wounds

### Severity and Trauma

Each wound row has a severity, and the severity sets the **Trauma** the wound gives.

| Severity | Trauma | What it means |
|---|---:|---|
| Minor | 0 | A short shock: a round or less. |
| Serious | +1 | A real injury. Heals after 1 Long Rest. |
| Severe | +2 | A bad injury. Steps down to a lighter one after 1 Long Rest and heals after 2. |
| Critical | +2 | Lose the next Action Stage, and a Death Save in d4 turns unless someone stabilizes you first. Needs real medical care. |
| Catastrophic | +4 | Immediate Death Save. |

Trauma adds up.
When it reaches the class's threshold, the character rolls on the Death Table (see "Trauma and the Death Table").

### Which wound table

| Attack | Wound table |

### Wound terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Now / Lasting** | "Now" effects last a round or less. "Lasting" effects start the next round and stay until the wound heals. |
| **Lose the next Movement Stage** | You cannot move next round. |
| **Lose the next Action Stage** | No attack, no Defense, no reload and no other action next round. |
| **Dazed** | Lasts one round: you lose the next Movement Stage and the next Action Stage. Dazed alone has no lasting penalty and no Trauma. |
| **Knocked down** | You fall down. See "Standing up from prone". |
| **Knocked unconscious** | You are Out of the fight. |
| **Minor Concussion** | Dazed, and -10 Handle until a Long Rest. |
| **Severe Concussion** | Dazed and -40 Handle. After one Long Rest the penalty steps down to -10; after a second it clears. The step-down does not make you Dazed again. |
| **Half Movement** | Halve the distance you can walk or run. A wound that limits movement gives half Movement and no Evasive Movement, with no Body Save. |
| **No Evasive Movement** | You cannot choose Evasive Movement. |
| **Next attack / next roll / next Check at Disadvantage** | The very next roll of that kind is at Disadvantage, then the effect ends. "With that arm" or "with that hand" means the next roll using it. |
| **Disadvantage on Checks involving exertion** | Apply Disadvantage to those Checks as written. |
| **Strenuous exertion impossible** | You cannot do it, as written. |
| **Handle penalty** (such as "-20 Handle") | Take that much off Handle for the actions the row names, for as long as it says. It makes the action harder, never impossible. |
| **Dropped unless a Handle Check succeeds** | Make a Handle Check; on a failure, whatever that hand holds falls to the ground. |
| **Bleeding timer** | +1 Trauma in d4 turns unless someone stabilizes the wound with First Aid first. A bleeding wound gives its extra Trauma once. |
| **Death Save in d4 turns unless stabilized** | Roll on the Death Table when the timer runs out, unless someone stabilizes you first. |
| **Immediate Death Save** | Roll on the Death Table now, whatever your Trauma. Being a Tough does not delay it. |
| **Stabilize / First Aid** | Takes an action and hands on the casualty, from someone with First Aid or better. It stops a bleeding timer or a Death Save countdown. It does not remove the wound or its Trauma. |
| **Real medical care** | A doctor or a hospital, beyond First Aid. |
| **Crawl only without help** | You cannot stand or walk; you can crawl, or be carried or helped along. |
| **Reduced** | A dislocated joint put back in place. |
| **Dominant / other hand** | Some hand wounds hit harder on your dominant hand. If it is not settled which hand was hit, roll d4: 1-2 the other hand, 3-4 the dominant hand. |
| **Long Rest** | A full night's sleep somewhere reasonably safe. |

> **Not yet decided:**
> the exact meaning of Long Rest, "until recovered", "physical Check" and how long being knocked unconscious lasts (until decided, read them as above, "until the GM judges it healed", "a Strength, Speed or Handle Check", and "the rest of the fight");
> whether a wound that rules out sprinting or exertion also rules out melee swings while still allowing gunfire;
> whether handedness becomes something every character records;
> whether First Aid needs a Check to stabilize.

### Wound Checks and Saves

Some rows ask the target for a Body Save or a Handle Check.
The target rolls it right away: d100 at or under their Body or Handle as it stands now.
The row says what success and failure do.
A failed Save gives +1 Stress, and a failed Stat Check gives the class's usual Stress, as always.

### Stacking wounds

Wound results stack: each new wound adds its own Trauma and effects.
Trauma always adds up.

> **Not yet decided:** how Disadvantage stacks across wounds (one pair of dice, or more), and whether Handle penalties from different wounds add up or only the worst counts.

## Out

### Going Out

**Out** means a character is no longer in the fight.
It is not only for dodgeball.
A character goes Out when:

- they leave the fight (see "Leaving the fight");
- a wound knocks them unconscious;
- a Dodgeball row puts them Out;
- they throw an object marked "catching this puts the thrower out", such as a dodgeball, and it is caught, or a Dodgeball row puts the thrower Out;
- the GM takes them out of the fight, for any reason the story calls for.

Going Out happens at once, not next round.
A character who is Out does not move, declare, attack or defend, and nobody can attack them.
Their declaration for the round is struck.
If they were in the middle of an attack, it stops; anything they had thrown drops to the ground.
If they were the target of an attack that has hit but not yet been defended, they roll no Defense, so the hit lands.
If they were about to catch a throw, it drops, and the attack is still stopped.

### Coming back in

The GM can bring a character who is Out back into the fight.
Their wounds, effects and initiative stay as they were.
If they come back before declaring starts, they declare as normal.
Once declaring has started, they make no attack and roll no Defense for the rest of that round.

## Grappling, takedowns and disarming

### Takedowns

A takedown is an opposed Strength Check (see "Opposed Checks").
The winner puts the opponent on the ground and starts on top.

If someone tries to grapple you or take you down while you attack them, your attack roll is at Advantage, but your Strength roll against the takedown is at Disadvantage.
The grappler rolls Strength normally.
The strike and the takedown happen at the same time.

### Ground position

- On top, your Handle attacks are at Advantage.
- On the bottom, your Handle attacks are at Disadvantage.
- The character on the bottom may attack with fists or try a Strength reversal.
- If the top character attacks while the bottom character tries a reversal, the top character rolls the opposed Strength Check at Disadvantage; the bottom character rolls normally.
- If the top character gives up their attack only to keep control, they roll the opposed Strength Check at Advantage.
- The attack and the reversal happen at the same time: a top character can land a hit and still lose the position.
- If both opposed Strength rolls fail, the position holds.

Being knocked down is not a ground fight: nobody holds you down unless they declared a grapple.

### Weapons in a ground fight

- Long weapons such as bats cannot normally be used while fighting on the ground.
- Short weapons such as knives can be used in a ground grapple.
- The character on the bottom cannot use weapons, but can still attack with fists.

### Disarming

Declare a Disarm: give up your attack to try to take a held weapon away.
The weapon-holder still resolves the attack they declared.
The disarm is an opposed Handle Check (see "Opposed Checks").
A successful weapon attack still lands even if the weapon is disarmed in the same exchange.
If the rolls and Handle are exactly tied, the weapon-holder keeps the weapon.

## Races and chases

### Racing someone to a place

A race to an object or a place is one opposed Speed check: each runner rolls d100 at or under their Speed.

- If one runner succeeds and the other fails, the one who succeeded gets there first.
- If both succeed, the higher roll gets there first.
- If both fail, they get there at the same time.

Being closer or further away is handled at the table, for example with Advantage or Disadvantage on the roll.

Example: Mirelda (Speed 58) and Rex (Speed 45) both dive for a dropped bat.
Mirelda rolls `31` and Rex rolls `40`.
Both succeed, and Rex's roll is higher, so Rex gets to the bat first.

### Chases

A chase is run on a **clock**: a track of segments that fills up and empties.

1. The GM sets the clock's size by the terrain, at most 10 segments: an open field is 10, hilly woods with cover nearby might be 5.
2. Each segment is one unit (10 meters) of gap between pursuer and quarry. The clock starts at the actual gap.
3. Each round both sides make an opposed Speed roll. A successful roll gives as many units as its tens digit (Speed 65 rolling `40` gives 4 units); a failed roll counts as 0.
4. The gap moves by the difference: quarry 5 units, pursuer 3 units opens the gap by 2.
5. At 0 the pursuer catches the quarry. When the clock is full, the quarry escapes.

## Worked examples

### Example: a round with a bat

Tommy (Handle 52, Scrapping, Strength 47) holds a baseball bat.
Rex (an NPC, Handle 45) has a pocketknife.
Initiative put Tommy first and Rex second, so Rex declares first.

1. **Rex declares** (he is slower): he stays put and will stab Tommy, with a 10 Defense line against Tommy. His Handle pool is 45, so his attack number is 35.
2. **Tommy declares**, having heard Rex: he stays put and will swing at Rex, with a 20 Defense line against Rex. His pool is 52 + 10 (Scrapping) = 62, so his attack number is 42.
3. **Movement resolves:** nobody moves.
4. **Tommy attacks first.** He rolls `36`, under 42: a hit with Quality 3.
5. Rex rolls Defense with his 10 line: `07` is under 10, a success. Its tens digit is 0, plus 1, so it takes 1 off. Quality 2 is left.
6. **Hit location:** blunt swing at a standing target, d100 `23`: the head. Tommy could move the hit up to 2 steps, but keeps it on the head.
7. **Strength Roll:** Tommy rolls `52` against Strength 47, a failure, so it adds nothing.
8. **Impact** = 2 (Quality) + 1 (bat) + 0 (Strength) - 0 (moved) - 0 (no helmet) = 3.
9. **Wound:** Blunt Force, head, Impact 3: Eye / face injury, Serious, +1 Trauma. Rex is Dazed next round and has -20 Handle on ranged and visually precise actions until a Long Rest.
10. **Rex attacks second.** His wound starts next round, so he still stabs this round. He rolls `29`, under 35: Quality 2. Tommy's 20 line rolls `25`, a failure, so nothing comes off.
11. **Hit location:** stab, d100 `48`: the torso. Rex keeps it there.
12. **Strength Roll:** Rex has Strength 40 and rolls `18`, adding 1.
13. **Impact** = 2 + 0 (pocketknife) + 1 = 3. **Wound:** Piercing, torso, Impact 3: Puncture between the ribs, Serious, +1 Trauma, with a bleeding timer. This is round 1 and the timer's d4 comes up 2: unless someone stabilizes Tommy with First Aid before the end of round 3, he takes another +1 Trauma.

Next round, Rex is Dazed: he cannot move, and he has no attack and no Defense.

### Example: a dodgeball caught

Rex (Handle 40, no Throwing Arm) throws a dodgeball at Mirelda.
Mirelda declared a 30 Defense line against Rex and has a free hand.

1. Rex's attack number is 40. He rolls `25`: a hit with Quality 2.
2. Mirelda rolls Defense with her 30 line: `21`, a success. It takes off 2 + 1 = 3, so the Quality is now -1. The throw is stopped.
3. She has a free hand and a dodgeball has a 50% catch chance, so her catch chance is 50% + 10 for the one point below 0 = 60%.
4. She rolls `44`, under 60: she catches it. A dodgeball puts the thrower Out when caught, so Rex is Out.

### Example: shooting at someone moving evasively

Mirelda (Speed 58) declares Evasive Movement, which uses her action, so she has no attack and no Defense this round; Rex (Handle 45, no Firearms) declares a shot at her with a .38 revolver.

1. **Movement resolves:** Mirelda rolls Speed, `47`, a success: ranged attacks at her this round take -20.
2. **Rex's turn:** the GM calls Medium range and, since the revolver has no range numbers yet, sets the modifier at +0. His attack number is 45 + 0 - 20 = 25.
3. He rolls `18`, under 25: a hit with Quality 1. Defense does not work against guns, so it stands.
4. **Hit location:** firearm, d100 `30`: the torso. Rex keeps it there. A gun makes no Strength Roll.
5. **Impact** = 1 + 1 (revolver) = 2. **Wound:** Gunshot, torso, Impact 2, Serious, +1 Trauma. The row says roll d2: a 1 is a Flesh wound, a 2 a Bleeding wound.

### Example: Minimum Strength

Mirelda (Strength 31) picks up an axe, which has Minimum Strength 35.
31 is below 35, so she takes -10 Handle: any shortfall costs at least -10, and 31 is still in the 30s.
With Strength 19 she would take -20, and with Strength 8, -30.

## Design principles

These are the ideas the rules are built on.
When the rules do not cover something, rule in their spirit.

- Keep the system lean, with enough texture that character abilities matter.
- Wide Stats and strong weaknesses are desirable. A naturally weak, untrained kid should be clearly different from a trained adult fighter.
- Low ability should matter without long runs of boring, empty combat rounds.
- High Handle should give more than accuracy: it should give room to attack while defending.
- Movement should change tactical options. Injuries such as half Movement can decide whether a melee attacker can close with a ranged opponent.
- Defense and Evasion make clean contact harder; they do not reduce the consequence of a bullet that already hit.
- Physical Stats should show in the story of a fight: a character's body matters to how violence unfolds.
- Successful attacks produce concrete consequences, not abstract hit points.
- Serious and Severe wounds change the fight at once and keep mattering afterward.
- Use a roll-twice condition for brief impairment, and fixed penalties for real, ongoing injury.
- Teenage fistfights can be dramatic, painful and consequential without routinely becoming lethal.
- Escalating to a dangerous weapon makes lethal wounds possible at once.
- Rare combat can carry some crunch, as long as it stays easy to remember after several sessions without a fight.
- Family supplies stakes and connections; class supplies a broad posture; skills supply specific competence.
- Class does not dictate personality, and Family does not dictate class.
- A player character's courage and surrender are the player's choice, unless something involuntary such as Panic or unconsciousness takes over.
- Mechanics serve the story; roleplay is not scorekeeping for rewards.

## What these rules do not cover

Monsters stay in the game, but how they work, including how they fight, is not decided.
For anything not covered here, the GM rules at the table.
